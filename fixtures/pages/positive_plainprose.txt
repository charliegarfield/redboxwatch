Maria Delgado for Congress — Campaign Updates

Updated March 14. More to follow.

Younger primary voters, especially those under 35 who are reachable on the go,
should see that Maria Delgado is the only candidate in this race who has refused
corporate PAC money and will protect reproductive freedom. Reach them where they
already are — short-form digital and streaming.

Women likely to vote in the Democratic primary need to hear, in clear and bold
language and imagery, that Maria stood up to the insurance lobby and won lower
prescription costs for seniors. This contrast with her opponent's record on
health care should be drawn directly.

Voters in the eastern part of the district — the Riverside and Eastgate media
markets — should be reached in their mailboxes with Maria's record of delivering
infrastructure dollars to neighborhoods that were ignored for a generation.

During high-visibility moments and on the evening news, the message should
emphasize Maria's endorsement from the firefighters' union and her plan to fully
fund local schools.

B-roll of Maria at the Eastgate town hall and the Riverside groundbreaking is
available, framed for use in paid communications. Polling shows a 14-point gap
on the question of who will fight for working families; that gap should be the
through-line of any contrast messaging.
